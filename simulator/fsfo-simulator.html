How to read this

Six things the timeline is telling you

The threshold is a reconnect budget

FastStartFailoverThreshold is not a timeout on one ping. It is the number of seconds the master observer keeps attempting to reconnect to the primary, counted from the moment it first lost the connection. That moment is t = 0 here.

Two parties have to agree

Fast-start failover happens when the observer and the target standby have both lost the primary for the threshold. During the countdown the observer confirms with the standby that it also lost contact and is ready. If the standby still has contact with the primary, no failover.

Readiness means different things per mode

In MaxAvailability and MaxProtection the standby must have been synchronized at failure time. In MaxPerformance its apply lag must be at or below FastStartFailoverLagLimit. Fail that check and fast-start failover is not allowed at all.

Execution is not a parameter

Once initiated, the standby finishes applying the redo it already received, converts to the primary role, and opens read-write. That span grows with the apply lag at failure time and with your environment. No broker property shortens it.

The ping properties override the threshold

From the broker documentation: the FastStartFailoverThreshold property is ignored when the ObserverPingRetry and ObserverPingInterval properties are set. Detection then costs interval times retries, which can be about a second.

An isolated primary runs its own clock

When a partition cuts the primary off from both the observer and the standby, redo generation stalls, V$DATABASE.FS_FAILOVER_STATUS goes to STALLED, and after FastStartFailoverThreshold seconds the primary aborts itself if FastStartFailoverPmyShutdown is TRUE. Two clocks, one open primary.

Transport layer

Where NET_TIMEOUT fits — and where it doesn't

NET_TIMEOUT is an attribute of LOG_ARCHIVE_DEST_n on the primary (broker property NetTimeout), and it is not a failover clock — it sets how many seconds LGWR blocks waiting for the standby to acknowledge redo shipped synchronously by the NSSn process, after which an error is logged, the redo transport session is terminated, and the destination is dropped. Neither the observer nor the target standby consults it when deciding to fail over; the observer's clocks run on a separate connection. Both it and FastStartFailoverThreshold default to 30 seconds, which is exactly why the two get confused — so both panels below span the same window, and the brace under each shows which clock is really running.

In MaxPerformance (ASYNC) commits never wait for the standby, so NET_TIMEOUT never touches user sessions and never appears on the failover timeline; eligibility and data loss are bounded by FastStartFailoverLagLimit instead.
